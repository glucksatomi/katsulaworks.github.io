THE POETICS OF PATTERN

展示について

About the Exhibition

本展では、染色工芸家・西片玖美子（1946–2021）が遺した作品や型紙、図案、制作資料を整理しながら、その多彩な創作の世界を紹介していきます。

西片玖美子の染色の原点には、芹沢銈介との出会いがありました。高校時代、日本民藝館でその作品に触れたことが、のちに大学で紅型を研究し、卒業後に沖縄へ渡って紅型を学ぶ契機となります。しかしその後の西片は、特定の様式や流派にとらわれることなく、独自の文様や柄を次々と生み出していきました。心惹かれる世界があれば、その源までさかのぼって学び、自分のかたちとして描き直す。古典的な文様から、身近な花や鳥、虫、風景、そして自由な幾何学模様まで、作品に登場するモチーフの幅広さは、そうした軽やかな興味の移ろいの軌跡でもあります。

西片玖美子の作品の多くには、見る人が思わず「かわいい」と感じる愛らしさがあります。そしてその目で、改めて西片の古典的な柄を眺めると、端正な文様のなかにも、同じ性質がにじんでいることに気づかされます。

桂舎が注目するのは、受け継がれてきた文様を大切にしながらも、それらを独自に組み合わせ、身のまわりのものに新しい形を見つけ、遊ぶように自由な表現へと展開していく、生き生きとした創造性です。西片玖美子にとって文様は、見つけて、遊ぶことのできる、至福の世界だったのかもしれません。

西片玖美子の生誕80周年にあたる2026年、桂舎と、作品・資料・目録を管理する「空眠（Koomin）」の共催により、本展を開催します。西片玖美子の作品から、「受け継ぐ」「組み合わせる」「見つける」「遊ぶ」という四つの視点を手がかりに、オンライン企画展を開催し、あわせて年賀状や型紙などに焦点を当てた小さな特別展示も随時公開していきます。

主催：桂舎、空眠

This exhibition explores the richly varied creative world of Kumiko Nishikata (1946–2021), a Japanese textile artist, through her works, stencils, original designs, and archival materials.

Nishikata’s journey into textile dyeing began with an encounter with the work of Keisuke Serizawa. As a high school student, she came across his work at the Japan Folk Crafts Museum. This experience later inspired her to study bingata, the traditional Okinawan dyeing technique, at university and, after graduation, to travel to Okinawa to learn the craft firsthand.

Over time, however, Nishikata developed a distinctive artistic language of her own, unconstrained by any particular style or school. Whenever something captured her imagination, she would explore its origins, study it closely, and reinterpret it in her own way. From classical patterns to familiar flowers, birds, insects, landscapes, and freely imagined geometric forms, the remarkable variety of motifs in her work reflects the ease and curiosity with which her interests moved from one subject to another.

Many of Nishikata’s works possess an irresistible charm—a quality that might best be described by the Japanese word kawaii, or “cute.” Once we recognize this quality, we begin to see it even in her more classical designs, where a similar sense of playfulness and affection quietly emerges from within their elegant, carefully composed patterns.

What particularly interests KATSULA WORKS is the lively creativity with which Nishikata embraced inherited patterns while freely combining them in unexpected ways, discovering new forms in the world around her, and transforming them into playful expressions of her own. For Nishikata, perhaps patterns were a world in themselves—a blissful world to explore, discover, and play within.

In 2026, marking the 80th anniversary of Kumiko Nishikata’s birth, KATSULA WORKS and Koomin, which manages her works, archival materials, and catalogues, jointly present this exhibition.

The online exhibition explores Nishikata’s work through four perspectives: Inheriting, Combining, Discovering, and Playing. Alongside the main exhibition, a series of smaller special presentations will be released periodically, highlighting such materials as her New Year’s greeting cards, stencils, and other aspects of her creative practice.

Organized by: KATSULA WORKS and Koomin

EXHIBITION 01

はじめての出会い

First Encounters

「受け継ぐ」「組み合わせる」「見つける」「遊ぶ」。この四つの視点から選んだ作品には、西片玖美子ならではの文様の楽しさが表れています。

制作された時代も、モチーフも、表情もさまざまです。古典の文様を端正に描いたものもあれば、身近なものから思いがけない形を見つけ出し、自由に遊ぶように生まれた文様もあります。その一つひとつに共通するのは、西片らしい、どこか愛らしいまなざしです。

何を美しいと感じ、何に心を動かされ、そこからどんな形を見つけ出すのか。文様には、つくり手が世界を見つめる、その人ならではの感性が映し出されているのかもしれません。

ここから始まるシリーズを通して、西片玖美子の文様の世界を少しずつひもといていきます。まずはこの12点のなかから、心にとまる文様を見つけていただければ幸いです。

The works have been chosen around four themes: Inheriting, Combining, Discovering, and Playing. Each reveals something distinctive about Nishikata’s approach to pattern-making.

Created at different times and inspired by a wide variety of subjects, these works are remarkably diverse in character. Some draw upon the refined elegance of classical patterns, while others transform familiar forms into unexpected, playful designs that bring a smile to the viewer’s face. Yet throughout them all, we find a certain tenderness and charm—a quality of kawaii, or endearing playfulness, that is unmistakably Nishikata’s own.

What do we find beautiful? What catches our attention, stirs our imagination, and inspires us to discover new forms? Perhaps patterns reveal something of the unique way in which an artist sees and experiences the world.

Through this series, we invite you to explore Nishikata’s world of patterns, one discovery at a time. We hope that among these twelve works, you will find a pattern that speaks to you.

「Kumiko Nishikata — The Poetics of Pattern」の第1回となる本展では、西片玖美子の文様の世界を初めて訪れる方にも、その豊かさと魅力を感じていただけるよう、12点の作品を選びました。

For First Encounters, the opening exhibition in the series Kumiko Nishikata — The Poetics of Pattern, we have selected twelve works that offer an introduction to the richness and diversity of Nishikata’s creative world, especially for those encountering her patterns for the first time.

THE ARTIST

西片玖美子（1946–2021）略歴

Kumiko Nishikata (1946–2021) — Biography

西片玖美子（1946–2021）略歴 1946年　東京都に生まれる。「板締め」から染色を始める 桜蔭学園高等学校在学中、日本民藝館で芹沢銈介の作品に出会う 早稲田大学第一文学部美術専修に進学。在学中、芹沢銈介に師事した村上元彦に型染を学ぶ 1969年　卒業論文「琉球紅型文様に於ける日本染織の影響について」。卒業後、アメリカ統治下の沖縄へ渡り、首里の金城昌太郎の紅型工房で約1年間学ぶ その後、祝まなに学ぶ。また高田装束研究所の高田倭男のもとで装束を学び、皇室の十二単や能衣裳の制作・復元に携わる 1974年　徳島県で藍染を研究。のちに自宅アトリエに藍甕を設える 着物、帯、のれん、屏風、掛軸の制作から、本の装丁、グリーティングカード、簪、ネクタイなどのデザインまで、幅広く手がける

1946 Born in Tokyo. Began exploring textile dyeing through itajime, a traditional clamp-resist dyeing technique.

While attending Oin Senior High School, encountered the work of Keisuke Serizawa at the Japan Folk Crafts Museum.

Went on to study art at Waseda University’s School of Humanities and Social Sciences. During her university years, studied katazome (stencil dyeing) under Motohiko Murakami, a former student of Serizawa.

1969 Graduated with a thesis titled “The Influence of Japanese Textiles on Ryukyuan Bingata Patterns.”

After graduation, traveled to Okinawa, then ruled under U.S. administration, and spent approximately one year studying bingata at the workshop of Shotaro Kinjo in Shuri.

Later studied under Mana Iwai. She also studied traditional court costumes under Shizuo Takada at the Takada Institute of Costume Research, participating in the creation and restoration of jūnihitoe (twelve-layered ceremonial court robes) for the Imperial Family and Noh costumes.

1974 Studied indigo dyeing in Tokushima Prefecture. Later installed an indigo dye vat in her home studio.

Her creative practice encompassed a remarkable range of works, from kimono, obi sashes, noren curtains, folding screens, and hanging scrolls to book covers, greeting cards, kanzashi hair ornaments, and neckties.
